Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 August 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how KMPS Global Corporation, an Ohio corporation doing business as Tejdux (“we”, “us”, or “our”), handles personal data in connection with the InfluencerCRM platform at www.tejdux.com (the “Service”). It should be read alongside our Terms of Service.

The most important thing to understand about this Service

InfluencerCRM is used by brands and agencies to manage relationships with content creators. Much of the personal data in the Service is data about creators, uploaded by a brand — often imported in bulk from a spreadsheet the brand already maintained.

For that data, the brand is the controller and we are only the processor. We hold it on the brand's behalf and act on their instructions. If you are a creator asking why a brand holds your details, Section 8 explains what to do — and we will help — but the brand, not us, decides what is collected and why.

2. Data we collect about account users

This applies to people who sign up to use the Service on behalf of a brand or agency.

  • Account details — email address, a securely hashed password, brand or account name, assigned role, and account type (brand or agency).
  • Social sign-in data — if you sign up with Google or Facebook, we receive your name, email address, and provider account identifier from that provider. We do not receive your password, and we do not post to your social accounts.
  • Invitation data — if you are invited to an account, the inviter's email address and the role offered. Invitation tokens are stored only as a hash, never in readable form.
  • Session data — authentication tokens issued when you sign in, and their expiry.
  • Support correspondence — messages you send us and our replies.
  • Billing data — where you subscribe to a paid plan, our payment processor Stripe handles your card details. We receive confirmation of payment and subscription status, not your full card number.

3. Data we collect about creators

This applies to creators who register a Creator Portal account directly with us.

  • Account details — email address, hashed password, and the social handles you provide.
  • Claims — records of the brand-held Creator Records you have asserted are you, and whether each was approved or rejected.
  • Session data — an opaque server-side session identifier. Creator sessions are separate from brand-side sessions and carry no access to any brand's wider data.

Creating a Creator Portal account does not, by itself, give a brand any new information about you, and does not require you to have any existing relationship with a brand on the Service.

4. Data brands import about creators

This section describes data we hold as a processor, on behalf of a brand. We did not choose to collect it and we do not decide what it is used for.

4.1 What it typically contains

A brand may import or manually enter records about creators they work with or wish to approach. These typically include name, social handle and platform, email address, campaign participation, fees and payment status, discount codes and affiliate links, notes and correspondence logs, and content the creator produced for that brand.

4.2 The brand's responsibility

Under our Terms of Service, every brand must represent that it has a valid legal basis for the data it uploads, that it obtained the data lawfully, and that it has given any notices and obtained any consents its own jurisdiction requires. Brands are also required not to upload special categories of personal data, and not to upload data relating to minors.

4.3 What we do with it

We store it, make it available to the authorised users of that brand's workspace, and process it to provide features the brand uses — such as organising it into campaigns, mapping spreadsheet columns during import, and recording attribution. We do not sell it, we do not use it to build profiles for our own purposes, and we do not use it to train machine learning models.

4.4 Automated column mapping during import

When a brand imports a spreadsheet, the Service can suggest how each column maps to a field. To do this it sends the column headings and the target field names to our AI provider, OpenAI, which returns suggested mappings.

The rows themselves are not sent.

Only the header text — for example “Instagram Handle”, “Fee Paid”, “Email” — leaves our systems for this feature. The creator records underneath those headings are not transmitted to the AI provider. Suggestions are advisory, and the person importing must review and confirm the mapping before any records are created.

9. If you are a creator

You may be in our systems without ever having signed up, because a brand added you to their workspace or imported a spreadsheet containing your details. This section is for you.

9.1 Where your data came from

Typically from the brand itself — a spreadsheet they already kept, details you gave them directly, a form you filled in, or a public social profile. We did not collect it from you, and we do not buy creator lists.

9.2 Who to contact

Because the brand is the controller of that data, they are best placed to answer why they hold it, correct it, or delete it. If you know which brand it is, contact them first.

If you do not know which brand holds your data, or a brand does not respond, contact us at privacy@tejdux.com. We will identify the relevant brand or brands and forward your request, and we will assist them in responding. Where we are legally permitted to act directly, we will.

9.3 Claiming your record

If you register a Creator Portal account, you can claim a brand's record of you. Once the brand approves the claim, you can see the collaborations and campaign participation associated with you in that workspace.

A rejected claim does not remove your legal rights.

Claim approval is a product feature that controls what you can see in the portal. It is not a gate on your data protection rights. If a brand rejects your claim, you may still exercise every right in Section 8 in respect of data held about you, and both we and the brand remain obliged to honour those rights. Our Terms require brands to handle claims in good faith and prohibit rejecting a legitimate claim in order to frustrate a rights request.

9.4 Objecting to being listed

If you do not want a brand to hold your details at all, you can object. Tell the brand directly, or tell us and we will pass it on. Where a brand relies on legitimate interests, a valid objection generally requires them to stop unless they can show overriding grounds.

11. Security

We use technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, including:

  • encryption in transit (HTTPS/TLS) and at rest;
  • passwords stored only as salted hashes, never in readable form;
  • invitation tokens stored only as hashes;
  • workspace isolation, so each brand's data is scoped to its own account;
  • separate authentication for creator portal sessions and brand-side sessions;
  • role-based access control within accounts; and
  • access to production systems limited to personnel who need it.

No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. If a breach affects your personal data and the law requires it, we will notify you and the relevant supervisory authority within the required timeframes. Where we act as a processor, we will notify the affected brand without undue delay so they can meet their own obligations.

To report a vulnerability or suspected incident, contact security@tejdux.com.

12. International transfers

Where we transfer personal data out of the UK, EEA, or another region with transfer restrictions, we rely on an appropriate safeguard — such as an adequacy decision, Standard Contractual Clauses, or the UK International Data Transfer Addendum.

13. Cookies and similar technologies

We use only what the Service needs to function. Strictly necessary cookies and equivalent browser storage keep you signed in, maintain your session, and protect against cross-site request forgery. These cannot be disabled without breaking sign-in.

14. Children

The Service is a business tool and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 18. Brands are prohibited from uploading data relating to minors. If you believe we hold a child's data, contact us and we will delete it.
