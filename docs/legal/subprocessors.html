Sub-processors

Last updated 23 August 2026

A sub-processor is a third party we engage to process personal data on behalf of our customers. This page lists every one, what it does, and what it receives. It is referenced by Section 7 of our Data Processing Addendum and by Section 7 of our Privacy Policy.

How to be told when this changes. We give at least 30 days' notice before adding or replacing a sub-processor, by email to your account's notification address. You may object on reasonable data protection grounds within that period — Section 7.3 of the DPA sets out what happens then, including a pro-rata refund if no solution can be found.

OpenAI, L.L.C. Suggesting how spreadsheet columns map to fields during an import Column headings and target field names only — for example “Instagram Handle”, “Fee Paid”. The rows beneath those headings are never transmitted. United States
Google LLC Business email, for support and service correspondence Your email address and the content of messages you send us United States
Why the import feature sends so little. Mapping a spreadsheet needs to know what a column is called, not what is in it. Sending the headings alone is enough for the suggestion to be useful, and it means a creator's name, email or fee never leaves our systems for this feature. The suggestion is advisory in any case — whoever runs the import reviews and confirms the mapping before any record is created.

Services that are not sub-processors

Some third parties receive data because you tell the Service to exchange data with them. They act as independent controllers under their own terms rather than on our instructions, so they are not sub-processors and are not covered by the notice commitment above. They are listed here because the distinction is easy to lose.
